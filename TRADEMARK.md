# Warmstart trademark policy

The Warmstart **code** is free software under [Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). The Warmstart **name**
is not part of that grant, and this page explains what that means in practice.

Apache-2.0 §6 grants copyright and patent rights but expressly does **not** grant trademark rights.
The name "Warmstart", the `warmstart.dev` domain, and the project's logo and visual identity remain
the property of Sunghwan Yoo.

None of this restricts what you may do with the code. It restricts only what you may call the result.

## What you may do without asking

- **Use the software**, for anything, including commercially. That is what the licence is for.
- **Fork and modify it**, publish your fork, and sell services around it.
- **Say what your thing is.** Nominative use is fine and welcome:
  - "a fork of Warmstart"
  - "built on Warmstart"
  - "compatible with Warmstart"
  - "imports Warmstart projects"
- **Write about it** — reviews, tutorials, comparisons, criticism. You do not need permission to name
  the thing you are writing about, including to say you dislike it.
- **Link to it** using the name.

## What needs permission

- **Naming your fork or derivative "Warmstart"**, or a name close enough to be confused with it. Please
  rename. This is the one thing this page exists to ask for.
- **Using the logo or visual identity** as the identity of *your* product, service, or company.
- **Implying endorsement, affiliation, or official status** — "official Warmstart build", "Warmstart
  certified", "Warmstart Inc." — where none exists.
- **Registering** the name, a confusingly similar name, or a matching domain or app-store listing, as
  your own mark.

## Why a permissive licence and a reserved name

So that the code can be used and adapted with as little friction as possible, while a person who
downloads something called Warmstart can still know where it came from. A fork is welcome; a fork that
is hard to tell apart from the original is a problem for the people downloading it, not just for the
project.

This is ordinary practice for open-source projects and is not a restriction on your freedom to use,
study, modify or redistribute the software.

## Asking

Anything not covered here, or a use you are unsure about: open an issue or email the address in the
repository's contact information. A yes is usually easy.
